A short diary entry from a KDA meetup in Melbourne.

After finishing my Semester 1 Honours presentation on 15 June, I travelled to Melbourne for a short break a few days later. While I was there, I had the chance to attend a **KDA (Korean Developers Australia) meetup** on Thursday afternoon at the Microsoft building in Southbank.

</div>

The session was more practical than I expected, and that made it especially valuable. One presentation showed how Harness Engineering is being used in real workplace workflows. Seeing the actual workflow and how these tools fit into an enterprise environment helped me understand how AI is being applied beyond tutorials or demos.

What stood out to me was that this kind of AI adoption is not limited to developers or computer science specialists. The direction seems to be that AI tools are raising the baseline for everyone in an organisation, not just developers or technical specialists.

If AI tools become easy enough for everyone to use, then simply having a Computer Science or Software Engineering degree may not be enough by itself in the future. It made me wonder whether deeper foundations in areas like mathematics, science, domain knowledge, or research could become an even stronger advantage.

Another presentation that caught my attention was about the **agent window** concept in VS Code. I already use CLI agents and IDE-based coding agents, but as agentic coding tasks become longer and more parallel, managing everything in a single chat can become messy. The idea of separating work into different sessions or windows felt like an interesting UX direction.

It is not just about making the model smarter. It is also about designing better interfaces for how humans supervise, organise, and collaborate with AI agents. That was one of the most interesting parts for me.

Overall, the meetup was genuinely helpful. Listening to presentations from senior people working in the industry was a valuable experience, and I am grateful for the time and insights they shared.

It also reminded me that these kinds of opportunities often come from unexpected connections. I happened to meet people and hear ideas that I probably would not have encountered otherwise. Hopefully, one day, I can also share my own experience with others in a way that is useful to them.
