## Why I Chose AI-901

After receiving the voucher, I had to decide which Microsoft certification would be the most useful for me.

I am not currently using Azure services in a full-time business or production environment, so I did not want to choose an advanced certification just because it sounded more impressive. I wanted something that matched my current background while also helping me understand Microsoft's AI ecosystem.

The Azure AI Fundamentals certification has also recently moved from the older AI-900 exam to the revised **AI-901** exam. The new exam still covers foundational AI concepts but also places greater emphasis on implementing AI solutions through **Microsoft Foundry**.

That sounded like a good fit for me.

I decided that AI-901 would be a useful bridge between the AI knowledge I already had and the Azure ecosystem I wanted to understand better.

## Taking the Exam in Perth

Today, I went to Perth city and took the exam at a Pearson VUE test centre.

I can't really say much about the exam for confidentiality reasons, but I had enough time to work through it without feeling rushed.


The two broad areas were:

- **Identify AI concepts and capabilities**
- **Implement AI solutions by using Microsoft Foundry**

The first area covers the general concepts behind AI workloads, including generative AI, machine learning, natural language processing, speech, computer vision, information extraction, responsible AI, and agents.

The second area is where knowing the Microsoft ecosystem becomes more important. It is not enough to understand what computer vision or natural language processing means in general. You also need to recognise how Microsoft Foundry and its related tools can be used to build those solutions.

Anyone preparing for the certification should check the official [Study guide for Exam AI-901: Microsoft Azure AI Fundamentals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-au/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/ai-901).

Microsoft updates certification content over time, so the study guide is a much better source than relying only on old videos or notes made for AI-900.

## How I Prepared

My preparation was fairly straightforward.

I mainly completed the two Microsoft Learn paths recommended for AI-901:

1. [AI concepts for developers and technology professionals](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/paths/ai-concepts/)
2. [Get started with AI applications and agents on Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/paths/get-started-ai-apps-agents/)

The first learning path covers the main AI concepts and terminology. It includes generative AI and agents, computer vision, speech, natural language processing, and information extraction.

The second path is more focused on building AI applications with Microsoft Foundry. It introduces the Azure side of the certification and shows how the different services and workloads fit together.

These two paths contained almost all of the knowledge I needed for the exam.

I also checked the **exam sandbox** before exam day. The sandbox lets you become familiar with the exam environment before sitting the real test. This was useful because I could focus on the questions during the exam instead of spending time wondering how the interface worked.

The final part of my preparation was the free practice assessment available through AI Skills Navigator.

You can find the certification page here:

[Microsoft Certified: Azure AI Fundamentals on AI Skills Navigator](https://aiskillsnavigator.microsoft.com/credentials/cert-83587e0a0754cfee561ade3e27d9fa1cdaf15ae03be52d2413b2b858d1b4eda4)

The practice assessment had 50 questions and showed my performance across the different topic areas.

My first attempt was pretty bad.

That result was actually helpful because it showed that knowledge of general AI concepts alone was not enough. Most of the areas I needed to improve were related to Azure services, Microsoft Foundry, and choosing the right Microsoft tool for a particular workload.

I went back through the Microsoft Learn material, reviewed the questions I had misunderstood, and made short notes on the Azure-specific areas that I kept mixing up.

I then completed the practice assessment a second time.

So my full preparation was basically:

- complete the two Microsoft Learn paths
- check the exam sandbox
- take the practice assessment
- review the weak areas
- take the practice assessment again

After that, I sat the real exam and passed.

## My Advice for Other Students

For a student with a computing background or who has already taken units like machine learning or AI, the fundamental concepts should be manageable.

The main challenge is learning the Microsoft-specific side.

I would focus on understanding:

- what Microsoft Foundry is used for
- which services support different AI workloads
- how models, agents, tools, and endpoints fit together
- the difference between general AI concepts and their Azure implementations
- which service is appropriate for a particular scenario

It is better to understand the purpose of each service than to memorise a long list of names without context.

## Comparing It with AWS Certified AI Practitioner

Both certifications test broad AI knowledge together with the services and terminology of a particular cloud ecosystem.

The AWS certification helped me understand how AWS presents AI, machine learning, foundation models, responsible AI, and generative AI services. AI-901 covered many similar foundational ideas, but through Microsoft's ecosystem and with an emphasis on Microsoft Foundry and the practical implementation of AI solutions.

For me, the best way to think about both exams is:

> The AI concepts transfer, but the cloud vocabulary changes.

Once you understand machine learning and AI fundamentals, the remaining task is learning how each provider packages those ideas into its own products and workflows.
