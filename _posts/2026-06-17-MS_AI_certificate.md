A short reflection on joining Microsoft AI Skills Fest 2026, exploring agentic AI learning content, and booking the Microsoft Azure AI Fundamentals exam with a free certification voucher.

## How I Found the Event

A few weeks ago, I was reading someone's LinkedIn post about Microsoft certifications. I clicked through out of curiosity, and that eventually led me to an event banner for **Microsoft AI Skills Fest**.

The timing caught my attention because the event was starting on **8 June 2026**. Around that period, I was quite busy preparing for my Semester 1 Honours presentation and a team meeting presentation for my 3D heart segmentation project. Still, since the event was free and seemed useful for continuous learning, I decided to register and explore it whenever I had time.

I have been trying to keep learning outside of university coursework as well, especially around AI, machine learning, computer vision, and cloud platforms. So this felt like a good opportunity to see how Microsoft was presenting practical AI skills and developer workflows.

## Exploring AI Skills Fest

The event included curated learning content across different AI and developer topics. I mainly focused on content related to **agentic AI**, **GitHub**, **GitHub Copilot**, and **Azure DevOps**.

What I liked was that the learning experience was not only made up of videos. Some parts were structured like guided tutorials, where Microsoft and GitHub resources walked through concepts step by step. I found this useful because it felt closer to how developers actually learn new tools: not just by listening, but by following along, reading examples, and seeing how the workflow is meant to be used.

The GitHub-related learning paths were especially interesting to me because they connected well with the current direction of AI-assisted software development. Instead of treating AI as just a chatbot, the content showed how tools like GitHub Copilot can be customized with instructions, prompts, and workflow patterns.

That felt very relevant to the way software development is changing. Developers are not only writing code directly anymore; they are also learning how to guide AI tools, review outputs, define project standards, and design workflows where AI can support planning, implementation, and review.

## A Nice Surprise: Certification Voucher

One of the nice surprises from the event was that completing eligible learning content could give participants a chance to claim a **free Microsoft certification exam voucher**.

I felt pretty lucky about this. Certification exams can be expensive for students, so having the opportunity to sit one for free is a real benefit. After completing the required learning content and submitting the voucher claim form, I later received the Pearson VUE voucher email.

I used the voucher to book **AI-901: Microsoft Azure AI Fundamentals** at a Pearson VUE test centre. After applying the AI Skills Fest voucher, the checkout price became **USD 0.00**.

## Why I Chose Azure AI Fundamentals

Last year, I completed the **AWS Certified AI Practitioner** certification. That experience helped me broaden my understanding of AI and machine learning in the AWS ecosystem, especially around foundation models, responsible AI, security, governance, and practical AI use cases.

This time, I decided to go with **Microsoft Certified: Azure AI Fundamentals** through the new **AI-901** exam.

For my current stage, this felt like the most suitable choice. I am currently a Computing Honours student, and I am interested in AI, machine learning, computer vision, software development, and future graduate roles. Since I already had an AWS AI certification, learning the Microsoft Azure AI side felt like a natural next step.

I considered whether I should try a more advanced certification, such as AI-102 or AI-103. However, those exams appear to require more hands-on Azure development experience with services such as Microsoft Foundry, SDKs, APIs, and deployment workflows. They look useful, but I think they would make more sense later when I have more practical Azure project experience.

Azure AI Fundamentals feels like the right balance for now. It is not too broad like a general cloud fundamentals exam, and it is not too advanced for my current level. It directly connects to my AI interests while helping me understand Microsoft's AI ecosystem and terminology.

## Reflections on Big Tech Learning Opportunities

One thing I have been thinking about recently is how valuable these free learning opportunities can be for students.

For example, Google has offered AI-related student promotions, and Microsoft ran this AI Skills Fest with free learning resources and certification voucher opportunities. Of course, these programs also help companies grow their ecosystems and introduce more people to their platforms. That is part of the strategy.

But from a student perspective, I still genuinely feel grateful for these opportunities. Access to free tools, training, cloud credits, or certification vouchers can make a real difference when you are trying to learn continuously but also need to be careful with costs.

I am glad I clicked that LinkedIn post and followed the chain of links. It would have been easy to ignore the banner and move on, but this turned into a useful learning experience and a free certification opportunity.

## What's Next

My AI-901 exam is now booked for July 2026. Before the exam, I plan to go through the official Microsoft Learn materials, especially the content around Microsoft Foundry, responsible AI, AI workloads, agents, text analysis, speech, computer vision, and information extraction.

Since I already studied similar foundational AI concepts for the AWS Certified AI Practitioner exam, I expect the main challenge will be learning the Microsoft and Azure terminology. In other words, the concepts are familiar, but I need to understand how Microsoft names and organizes the services.

I am looking forward to taking the exam and seeing how it compares with the AWS AI Practitioner experience.
