Today, I saw the news I really didn't expect to hear so soon.

Back in September, I wrote about AI and mathematical research. At the time, I thought something like the **Riemann Hypothesis** would take much longer. You can check trillions of zeros, but that still doesn't prove a claim about *all* of them. I figured we would need a genuinely new mathematical idea, not just more computing power.

Now [Levent Alpöge says he and Claude have found a proof](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf). They've released an enormous formalisation in **Lean**, and [independent checks](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) are already giving the claim serious weight. It hasn't been fully understood or accepted by the mathematical community, but if the verification holds up, the challenge may shift from *finding* a proof to understanding and simplifying one that's already there.

I don't really know enough about Lean or number theory to judge the proof myself.

Still, one part caught my attention. I'd vaguely wondered whether the strange, irregular distribution of primes along a number line might make more sense through some higher-dimensional mathematical structure. And now the proposed approach seems to involve exactly that kind of unexpected connection, bringing together ideas from very different areas of mathematics.

What surprises me most isn't the sheer amount of computation. It's the possibility that AI can connect distant mathematical ideas and help develop a genuinely new proof strategy.

It also makes me think that **how people use these models** will matter more and more. The researcher, the questions they ask, and the tools they build around the model all seem just as interesting as the model itself.

And then there's the uncomfortable part. If capabilities this powerful end up controlled by just one company or one country, that could become genuinely dangerous.

About 2.5 months ago, I thought this kind of breakthrough was much further away. The proof still needs scrutiny, of course. But even getting to this point so quickly is pretty wild.
